### 1. Derivation

Fix the channel, average over data and noise, and let \(m=[n-d]_N\). For \(d>0\), with \(p_n\) the **received** noiseless pilot in time,
\[
E[r_nr_m^*\mid H]=p_np_m^*+
(1-\rho)\!\!\sum_{\ell_i-\ell_j\equiv d\pmod N}
h_i h_j^* e^{2\pi j(\nu_i n-\nu_jm)/N}.
\]
The inverse DAFT is unitary, so it preserves white covariance. The prefix phase is one for these parameters. Pilot–data cross terms average to zero, but the deterministic Zadoff–Chu pilot product survives at arbitrary lags and can reinforce a true peak or create a competing one. ### 3. Two changes before freezing

1. **Add a matched mechanism control at \(\rho=.15\).** Keep pilot, channel, noise, and per-path data power fixed. Give each path an independent coded data waveform. This removes the expected cross-path data tone while preserving pilot ridges. Similar capture in both arms would undermine attribution to shared data.
2. **Score reference-relative candidates at a common false-candidate budget.** Keep the true anchor for this test, count non-anchor pairs as competitors, and allow each resolved peak to credit at most one path. This makes R2 answer whether autocorrelation supplies additional usable candidates at the same cost.

### 4. Prior art

Tsatsanis and Giannakis used second-order statistics for blind identification of basis-expanded time-varying channels. Cordes et al. give a direct precedent for the geometry: interfering ray pairs appear at differential delay and Doppler. Meng and Tugnait used superimposed training for time-varying channels, initializing from first-order statistics. [Tsatsanis & Giannakis, 1995](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACSSC.1995.540533) · [Cordes et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0407072) · [Meng & Tugnait, 2004](https://dihana.cps.unizar.es/proceedings/ICASSP/2004/pdfs/0300797.pdf)

A potentially new contribution would be showing that a **pilot-estimated reference** resolves enough pair ambiguities to recover missed paths and lower the pilot fraction required by the coded AFDM receiver. R1/R2 alone cannot establish that result.
