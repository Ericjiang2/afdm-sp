## Design (frozen)
Leg-2 design (MIXED8, ρ .5, 7 dB, byte-identical code) on fresh frames 2800–5199; arms F0, CD, Z (Γ = 0), H (hard decisions), S
(scalar, anchor), Z-sel. Δ_F = FER(Z) − FER(CD), Newcombe method-10 interval. Q1: lower > 0 → CD_BETTER, upper < 0 → Z_BETTER, else
NO_DIFFERENCE_SHOWN. Q2: inside ±1.5 pp → EQUIVALENT. Anchors (CD FER in [3%, 9%], S − CD ≥ 10 pp) first.

## Frozen verdicts (not relabelled)
Anchors **PASS** (CD 6.42%, S − CD +22.92 pp). Q1 **NO_DIFFERENCE_SHOWN**. Q2 **EQUIVALENT**.

## Interpretation (untested beyond what is stated)
1. **H** — On genuine close doublets, deleting Γ costs at most about 1.1 pp FER (upper bound), and the frozen rule calls the two
   equivalent at ±1.5 pp. The leg-2 "+1 pp" was not confirmed as a difference.
2. **H** — The exact co-delay block does keep genuine 0.35-bin doublets better: 58 frames where only CD fits both rays, against 21
   where only Z does, and on those frames Z leaves 19% of the doublet energy unfitted. This is a real but small advantage at the
   representation level; it moves FER by well under the margin.
3. **M** — The difference comes from support selection, not gain initialization: Z-sel equals Z exactly (4 : 4).
4. **M** — Hard decisions are slightly worse than CD (+0.67 pp, interval above 0 but inside the margin).
5. Phantoms stay negligible for every self-consistent arm (3–5 frames of 2,400; scalar 1,427).

## Consequence (proposal; Eric decides)
- Across the three legs: deleting Γ is equivalent on P1, phantom-free and FER-equivalent within ±1.5 pp on genuine doublets, and
  measurably worse at keeping close true doublets. `CODELAY` can be stated as the default self-consistent member with a demonstrated,
  small representation advantage on genuine close co-delay pairs; "irreplaceable" stays withdrawn.
