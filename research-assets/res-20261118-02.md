## Interpretation
1. **A full co-delay ban (`A2g`) fails in a genuine low-FER working regime when sparse doublets are present.** Unlike `Jakes-4` in
   [res-20261017-02](res-20261017-02.md) (FER `0.89–0.99`), here the receiver operates at `0/96` errors (`95%` upper bound `3.77%`), yet
   forbidding co-delay atoms leaves `14/96` frame failures unrescued while `FULL` and `CODELAY` rescue all `14` with zero harms and zero
   singleton phantoms on the four true singleton delays.
2. **`CODELAY` matches `FULL` on sparse doublets.** Both achieve `0/96` errors, `0` singleton phantoms, `37/42` two-atom triggered doublet
   taps (`16` at `0.35` bin, `21` at `0.75` bin), and `13.0381%` omitted doublet energy.
3. **Why `A2` and `A2n` did not lose FER in this cell (verified from `out/screen.jsonl`).** `MIXED8` has `6` true delays for `8` receiver
   slots (`2` surplus slots, zero missed weak delays at `12 dB, ρ = 0.5`): even when `A2` spent one swap on a singleton phantom (`8/21`
   triggers), its other swap resolved `21/21` of the `0.75`-bin doublets, sufficient for LDPC convergence at `12 dB`. Meanwhile `0.75` bin
   lies outside `A2n`'s `~0.60`-bin ban, and on `14/21` of the `0.35`-bin doublets `A2n` placed two legal wider-spaced atoms spanning
   `98%–99.7%` of the bandlimited tap energy. Together with [res-20261017-02](res-20261017-02.md) `R` (`32,000` scarce-slot single-ray
   keys where `A2` loses `17.8 pp` and `A2n` loses `3.3 pp` rescue), every fixed rule (`off`, `near-ban`, `full-ban`) fails in at least
   one low-FER working regime whereas `FULL` / `CODELAY` wins in both.

## Limits
- Exploratory 96-frame screen at a single calibrated operating point (`12 dB`, `ρ = 0.5`); `0/96` has an exact two-sided 95% upper bound
  of `3.77%`, so FER ordering below `10^{-2}` is not resolved within this dataset.
- Does not establish simultaneous single-channel FER superiority over `A2` and `A2n`; separation from `A2` and `A2n` relies on the
  scarce-slot `P1 / R` regime ([res-20261017-02](res-20261017-02.md)).
- `CODELAY` in `screen.py` was evaluated via the constant-variance `v̄ · 1` call path; the zero-variance-FFT closed-form kernel is
  qualified and benchmarked separately in [res-20261118-03](res-20261118-03.md).
