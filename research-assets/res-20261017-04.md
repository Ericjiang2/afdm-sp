## Interpretation
1. **The interpolation explanation is rejected.** With continuous Doppler there is no grid to interpolate, yet the scalar
   model keeps, and enlarges, its advantage. Its co-delay atoms become near-coincident pairs, the exact form called a
   "phantom" in E-1/E-2, and here they go with better NMSE (by 3.5 dB) and better FER.
2. **Refinement hurts the hard and full-Γ variants** (their FER floors rise to .02–.05 at 10–11 dB) and helps only the scalar
   one. Candidate explanations, both post hoc and untested:
   - (a) *Shrinkage.* The scalar Σv·I acts as an isotropic ridge that stabilises the fit of many atoms against
     decoded-data errors. The full Γ regularises only the coherent directions; hard and genie use only the ridge σ².
     Under that weaker regularisation, refinement overfits the data errors.
   - (b) *Full-Γ misspecification.* If the decoder APP moments are miscalibrated, for example over-confident wrong
     decisions, the exact EIV covariance is the wrong model and a cruder isotropic inflation is more robust.
3. **Consequence for the project's covariance claim.** Near-coincident co-delay pairs are harmful in CSTATE
   (res-20261017-01/-02: fixed 8 slots, so swaps displace true paths) and beneficial in SBL, where atoms are added without
   displacing anything. "Phantom atoms are a defect" is therefore a property of the fixed-budget swap search, not of the
   scalar data model. Across estimators the scalar model is not dominated.

## Limits
- One estimator (on-grid EM-SBL plus post-SBL coordinate refinement, not Luo's in-EM Taylor update), ρ .3 only, two
  scenarios, 200 frames per point, the P1 generator, the project's detector and decoder.
- Explanations (a) and (b) are untested. Two cheap discriminators: full Γ plus an added isotropic ridge Σv·I, which tests (a);
  and a calibration check of the APP variance against the empirical |x − μ|², which tests (b).
- The refinement accepts any strict decrease, so its acceptance rate includes floating-point-level moves; the FER readouts are
  unaffected.
