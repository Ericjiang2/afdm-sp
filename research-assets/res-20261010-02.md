## Observation
X1 (R4, T 20 vs 40, frames 0-199; g = Kc(T40)/Kc(T20), paired frame bootstrap):
- g decreases with rho at R1-R2. g(.1)/g(.3): 6.5 dB R1 1.39 [1.24, 1.57], R2 1.17 [1.11, 1.23]; 8 dB R1 1.25 [1.17, 1.34],
  R2 1.08 [1.01, 1.14]. Frozen P1 4/4 PASS.
- R1 optimum moves one grid step down with T40: 6.5 dB rho* .2 -> .1, 8 dB .3 -> .2. At R2-R4 the argmax does not move on the grid
  (P2 "<=" holds only trivially there). By R4 the coupling fades at 8 dB (g 1.14-1.16 for all rho); at 6.5 dB it persists
  (1.19 -> 1.07).
- 8 dB: rho .15 at T40 matches or beats rho .3 at T20 in every round (R1 170 vs 137, R4 238 vs 234). At equal T40, rho .15 loses
  12% Kc at R4 against rho .3 (238 vs 269).
- The data-aided scalar law predicted the direction (rho* falls with T). It does not predict magnitudes.
X2 (refit estimation error, white W vs genie-whitened GLS G vs achievable A; 2 SNR x 5 rho x 4 L, frames 0-199):
- Round 1 (pilot-only): r1 = eps_W/eps_G > 1 with CI in only 21/40 cells (P4 FAIL). GLS wins only in the noise-limited regime:
  up to 1.29 (8 dB rho .5 L8), at most 69% of the separable bound. It loses (r1 .88-.99) when K/S is above about .35-.65,
  where S = rho N/(sigma2 + 1 - rho). That includes the loads at which the R1-R4 cliffs sit. Never above the bound (P5 40/40).
- Round 2 (data-aided, receiver's own soft-symbol variances): GLS worse in 39/40 cells (r2 .85-1.00), including frames that decode.
- Achievable GLS recovers 70-100% of the genie gain where the genie helps (rho >= .5). Power mismatch of the receiver's nominal
  energy is negligible (W/Wt >= .986).

## Interpretation (scoped)
- Pilot power and intra-round joint budget are partial substitutes early in the schedule: extra inner updates let the receiver
  convert data energy into training energy, so less pilot is needed. The full-schedule optimum barely moves at 8 dB on this grid.
- Candidate mechanism for X2 (post hoc, untested): SP self-interference passes through the same channel as the pilot response.
  Along the true channel direction, GLS information is sum |(V^H p)_i|^2 s_i^2/(vbar s_i^2 + sigma2). That is no larger than the
  white value sum |(V^H p)_i|^2 s_i^2/(vbar E + sigma2) by Jensen. So whitening adds information everywhere except along h. Once
  the ridge bias along h dominates (high load), whitening loses.
- With WARM_1.0 (an exact round-1 start is forgotten), the initial-estimate slot — where LS-input refiners, including
  Transformer-based ones, operate — shows no headroom in this receiver: neither a perfect nor an optimally whitened start moves
  the cliff.

## Limits
Preliminary. One receiver family (genie delays, flat DPSS prior, K = 8L), QPSK, LDPC (3,6) n 2048, N 1024, two SNRs, coarse rho grid.
X1 compares T20/T40 only (no equal-compute (rho, R, T) frontier) and P2 is strict only at R1. X2 measures estimation error, not
FER. The round-2 whitening uses the receiver's own (possibly miscalibrated) variances. The Jensen mechanism and the K/S crossover
are post hoc. The earlier accepted whitening gain ([[res-20260610-01]], G0, F2-P support scoring) is a different receiver and
regime. Whether it lies in the noise-limited side of the crossover is unverified.
