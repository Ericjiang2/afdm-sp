## Interpretation (scoped)
Both variants are upper bounds that favour DDST: interference reduction phi applied to every data-uncertainty term (in the receiver
only the pilot-regressor share benefits), the distortion P_r x free to the detector, and Gaussian rather than QPSK input on the
retained dimensions. A PASS therefore only says DDST is not excluded at round 1 in this model. The dimension cost already removes
about 40% of the no-cost gain, and the optimal nulling rank (15–28% of N) is large, so the free-distortion assumption carries
real weight.

## Limits
- Round 1 only. At 6.5 dB rho .3 the DDST round-1 bound (86) stays below the baseline round-2/round-4 cliffs (134 / 168, res-20261009-01);
  a later-round gain is untested, and later rounds already remove data interference through data-aided refits.
- The law is a single-constant calibrated predictor (V2 rejected its mechanism); the decision uses a within-law ratio.
- No novelty: DDST (Ghogho et al. 2005) and DDST/SP with DPSS/BEM (He–Tugnait 2007–2008) exist; an AFDM DDST paper was not found in a partial search.
- Equal total power; PAPR and distortion-recovery cost not scored.

## Correction (2026-10-14, after the run, before any follow-up number)
The nulled subspace U was computed from each frame's true delay set, so both bounds also assume the transmitter knows the delay
support. This DDST-favouring assumption was not listed in the frozen protocol or in the Interpretation above. A transmitter without
delay feedback must null the subspace of the delay prior ({0..100}), which can only be larger. The follow-up protocol tests this
first (Stage 0 of `ddst-small-run-protocol-20261014.md`). The PASS-to-design decision stands as computed; its scope is "delay support
known at both ends".
