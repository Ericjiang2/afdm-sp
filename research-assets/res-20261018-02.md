## Post hoc (orchestrator; independent toy, not project code)
`posthoc/berkson_toy.py` sets up a data-aided dictionary with Berkson-type uncertainty: x = μ + u, with u independent of μ. The
support is the oracle one and includes two co-delay atoms with close Doppler; only the gains are estimated.

| mean v | LS on soft means (no Γ) | + Σv·I | + full Γ | true-data LS |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| .10 | **−18.1** | −8.2 | −16.5 | −26.9 |
| .34 | **−12.0** | −5.2 | −8.2 | −27.0 |
| .69 | **−6.3** | −2.5 | −2.9 | −27.0 |

When the dictionary is built from posterior means, the error x − μ is uncorrelated with the dictionary. Plain LS on B(μ) is then
unbiased, and adding any uncertainty covariance to the Gram shrinks the gains toward zero. The classical errors-in-variables
correction fixes measurement error of the classical type, X_obs = X + U. Here the error is of the Berkson type, X = X̄ + U with
U independent of X̄, and applying the classical correction introduces a bias instead of removing one.

## Interpretation (post hoc, scoped)
1. **This is unlikely to be an implementation bug in Γ or the E-step.** The EM is monotone, v ≡ 0 is exact, and an independent
   toy reproduces the direction. What is wrong is the objective: in this setting, adding the uncertainty covariance to the Gram
   over-shrinks the gains. A β-update bug is still not excluded by the monotonicity readout.
2. **Posterior misspecification is not needed to explain the E-3 reversal.** The reversal appears in the model-matched world with
   the same size. This bears directly on Codex's E-3c, whose misspecification arm loses most of its purpose.
3. **Candidate unification with E-1/E-2, untested.**
   - Γ's coherent structure is useful for *support decisions*: it removes the splitting incentive that creates phantom co-delay atoms.
   - Γ is harmful for *gain estimation* under Berkson-type uncertainty, because it over-shrinks.
   - CSTATE uses Γ only in the swap decision, and the detector re-fits gains jointly, so CSTATE benefits.
   - The SBL uses Γ for both decisions and gains and passes fixed gains to the detector, so the shrinkage dominates there.
   - Within that SBL, the scalar version is less damaged because splitting a path over m atoms cuts its shrinkage to s|h|²/m.
4. **Testable prediction.** Use Γ for support only and re-fit the final gains by LS on soft means without Γ. That variant should
   beat both S-diag and S-cov. A further arm with soft means and Γ = 0 throughout should beat S-diag in NMSE. Neither has been run.

## Limits
- One SBL receiver; ρ .3; 6–8 dB; the metric is NMSE only (no decoding in the synthetic world). In the synthetic world the
  posterior is independent of the observation noise (pure Berkson); in the real pipeline μ is computed from the same y with a
  round-1 channel estimate. Real data show the same direction and size, but the two settings are not identical.
- The toy uses oracle support and a simplified dictionary, so it shows a mechanism, not the size of the SBL effect.
- Novelty of "Berkson-type decoder uncertainty: use Γ for decisions, not for gains" is unchecked. Soft-data EM channel
  estimators commonly use X̄ᴴX̄ + Σ. Note that a properly iterated EM, where q(x) is recomputed with the current h from the same
  y, is not pure Berkson.

## Correction note (2026-10-18, explicit; original text above unchanged)
The post hoc Berkson reading above ("Γ in the Gram over-shrinks gains") was tested in [res-20261018-03](res-20261018-03.md)
(GSO, pre-registered) and is **refuted for this SBL pipeline**. Solving gains on the same selected atoms without Γ makes FER
much worse: under full Γ, .337 against .116. E-3c ([res-20261018-01](res-20261018-01.md)) is consistent with this: adding an
isotropic ridge to full Γ recovers 92% of the gap. In this pipeline the uncertainty term is a needed regulariser, and full Γ
is weaker than scalar Σv·I mainly because it under-regularises near-collinear difference directions. The toy remains valid
only for its own setting (oracle, well-conditioned support). This card's pre-registered SUSPECT verdict and its numbers stand.
