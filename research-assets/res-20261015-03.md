## Interpretation (scoped)
Embedded pilots cannot serve the main channel: the guard alone needs more than the whole frame. In a short-delay channel
where they fit, they trade 25% of the symbols for data energy and a guard-protected estimate. That makes EP robust at low
SNR, below the grid here. The SP receiver with CSTATE at rho .3 recovers its waterfall by about 5 dB and from there
delivers the full 1024 bits, 33% more than EP. With half the energy in the pilot (rho .5), SP loses below 6 dB. The SP
advantage depends on the design point. The rho .3 operating point found in P1 (and e_rho) is what makes SP competitive.

## Limits
- Scenario S is a constructed short-delay variant (delay law scaled by 25/102 and truncated; redraw rate 58.2%), with
  one Doppler law and per-frame unit path power.
- The grid starts at 4.5 dB, where EP is already at its cap. EP's low-SNR advantage region is not resolved, and the
  comparison does not cover SNRs below 4.5 dB.
- The comparison is at equal energy per frame. Per information bit, EP spends 1024/767 times the energy (+1.25 dB); a
  plot against Eb/N0 would shift EP right by that amount.
- rho_EP was tuned at a saturated dev point (.20 and .30 both error-free). The EP estimator is this project's
  implementation (threshold, successive cancellation, 0.05-bin refinement), not a published code. The EP detector is
  .4–.46 dB weaker than SP's TF detector, but this does not affect the labels (see above).
- One modulation, code family and N. sigma2 known.
