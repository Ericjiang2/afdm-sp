## Interpretation
1. **The estimation half of the split is false.** With the atoms fixed, replacing the full-Γ gain solve with an isotropic one
   hurts. This holds in the SBL (+.04 FER) and on CSTATE's doublet-rich A2 paths (−.03 rescue). The reading that full Γ
   under-regularises near-collinear difference directions does not describe how the gains behave.
2. **The decision result is now replicated prospectively.** On fresh frames, full-Γ swap decisions beat diagonal ones by .18
   rescue, the third independent confirmation after E-1 and E-2. It is the most robust mechanism finding of the line.
3. **What remains unexplained.** Scalar SBL (S-D) is still the best SBL variant (.055). The advantage does not come from a
   better gain step on given atoms, since it disappears once atoms are fixed. It must sit in the joint SBL dynamics, that is,
   in which atoms the scalar model selects and how SBL fits them together. One observation is consistent with that: each
   variant does best with gains from its own fit. On S-D's atoms, SBL gains beat Γ-free LS (GSO); on S-C's atoms, full-Γ gains
   beat isotropic ones (here). This is post hoc.
4. **Small reported effect.** For CSTATE, letting the detector re-estimate gains jointly (K-prod .292) is slightly better than
   fixing them at either solve (.277), −.014 [−.025, −.003].
