## Observation
- 40 instead of 20 inner updates per round move the FER .5 cliff in K at every round, in all 6 conditions tested
  (6.5 dB rho .2/.3; 8 dB rho .2/.3/.5; 9.5 dB rho .3): ratios 1.14-1.40 (R1), 1.08-1.21 (R2), 1.10-1.18 (R3-R4);
  paired frame-bootstrap CIs exclude 1. Gains saturate by T ~ 30-40 (T50/T40 = 1.00-1.01).
- 10 rounds (6.5 dB rho .3): Kc_R10 180.1 -> 213.6 (x1.186 [1.153, 1.221]); extra rounds add 8 in K at T = 20 and 16 at
  T = 40.
- Compute (6.5 dB rho .3, measured receiver time): the default R10/T20 (Kc 180) is dominated by R4/T30 (Kc 185 at ~55%
  of the compute) and R5/T40 (Kc 198 at ~83%). At equal inner-update budget, more rounds win up to ~80 updates and
  deeper inner loops win from ~120.

## Interpretation (scoped)
The round-1 inner loop is mid-transient at 20 updates (J12). Outer rounds iterate the return map but stall at the
fold/tunnel; the inner budget raises the per-round transfer and so moves the fold. Allocation should follow the
budget: rounds first, then inner depth.

## Limits
This is a configuration finding: the project's default (20 inner updates) was under-converged, and the compute
comparison is against that default, not a tuned published baseline. Not a method contribution on its own.
Single receiver family (genie delays, flat DPSS prior, K = 8L), QPSK, LDPC (3,6) n 2048, N 1024. The compute frontier was
measured at one condition, with timing from a 10-frame reference run (ranking robust, absolute seconds load-dependent).
Iteration scheduling in turbo / joint-CE receivers is a studied topic; novelty is unverified (targeted search pending).
The return-map prediction of the shift has not been made yet.
