**3) P10-vs-P8 checkpoint mystery**

My answer is: **the proposed diagnosis is directionally right, but incomplete.**

The “train on harder P=10 acts as regularization” story is plausible. The evidence you already have is consistent with it: the **P=8 retrained checkpoint has better `recall@6`**, yet is **strictly worse at BER** at 25/30 dB under the same non-F2-P protocol, including an outright **30 dB regression**, exactly where false additions are most expensive because the classical detector is already near-correct. 

But the sharper mechanism is not just “harder training regularizes.” It is:

**the deployed action is precision-critical and asymmetric, while the training proxy is recall-like and under-penalizes harmful adds.**
Under F2-P, especially with defer-prune and protected additions, a wrong high-confidence add can survive deeper into the pipeline and do real damage. So what the P=10 checkpoint may actually be learning is **better top-of-list calibration / abstention**, not necessarily a better representation in the abstract. The P=8 checkpoint may simply be **over-eager** in exactly the regime where extra recall is no longer useful.

So yes: **“harder regularization + proxy saturation” is broadly correct.** But I would rewrite it as **“precision/calibration mismatch under an asymmetric action cost, with P=10 shift acting as conservative regularization.”**

Is the planned **F2-P cross-eval** enough to validate that? **No. Necessary, not sufficient.**
It will tell you whether the bad P=8 checkpoint stays bad under the final protocol. It will **not** tell you why. To actually validate the diagnosis, add one tiny action-level audit at 25/30 dB:

* additions per frame,
* harmful-add rate,
* precision of protected additions after final prune,
* score/logit histogram for added atoms,
* precision@1 / precision@2 on the candidate pool.

If you can afford one more cross-check, run the reverse direction too: **P=8 ckpt on P=10**. If it under-adds and loses there, the “conservative calibration” story gets much stronger.
