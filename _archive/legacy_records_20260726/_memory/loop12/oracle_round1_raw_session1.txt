## 3) My answer to the P10-vs-P8 mystery

### Is your diagnosis correct?

**Partly yes, but your wording is too vague.**

The strongest version of the diagnosis is **not** just "train-harder regularization."
It is:

> **Harder training at P=10 appears to produce a more conservative / better-calibrated selector, and your proxy (`recall@6`, BCE) is blind to the failure mode that matters at high SNR: confidently adding the wrong atom when classical already nearly solved the frame.**

That is consistent with the same-protocol evidence:

* under the tau-cutoff protocol, **P10 is clearly more robust at 25/30 dB**,
* and those are exactly the SNRs where false additions are most damaging.

### What I think is definitely true

1. **There is a protocol confound in the current narrative.**
   P10 headline numbers were under **F2-P**; P8 was not. So part of the "P10 beats P8" story is contaminated.

2. **Even after controlling for protocol, a real high-SNR robustness gap may remain.**
   Your tau-cutoff apples-to-apples numbers already suggest that.

3. The likely mechanism is **calibration/selectivity**, not abstract "generalization" in the usual sense.

### What I would not claim yet

I would **not** claim you have proven "train-harder regularization" from the evidence shown so far. You have a good hypothesis, not a proof.

### Is planned F2-P cross-eval enough?

**No. Necessary, not sufficient.**

It is enough to answer the **operational** question: which checkpoint should you use?
It is **not enough** to validate the **mechanism**.

To validate the mechanism, you need one direct diagnostic on a shared P=8 test set at 25/30 dB:

* **learned-add count per frame**
* **false-add rate**
* **commit rate conditional on classical already containing the true support**
* ideally also **top-K vs K+1 margin / logit spread**

If P8-retrain shows:

* similar recall,
* **more adds**, or
* **higher false-discovery rate**,

then your diagnosis is basically closed.

So my bottom line is:

* **Operationally:** keep P10 as headline unless P8-under-F2-P clearly beats it.
* **Mechanistically:** F2-P cross-eval alone is **not enough** for a paper claim.
