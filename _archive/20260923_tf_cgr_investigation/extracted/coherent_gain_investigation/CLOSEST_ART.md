# Closest-art audit and remaining delta

## Parker and Schniter, Parametric Bilinear Generalized Approximate Message Passing

2015 preprint/2016 JSTSP. Inspected full text:
https://arxiv.org/html/1508.07575v2

Equations79-83 explicitly display cross reactions before neglecting off-diagonal terms.
Equation81 and AppendixI justify a particular simplification using independent zero-mean
random tensor entries in a large-system limit. Scalar-variance/structured implementation
sections also distinguish the corresponding diagonal contractions. Our deterministic
same-delay Gram terms do not obey that vanishing argument in a fixed-normalized-gap
family. This does NOT refute the theorem under its stated assumptions, and the restored
cross term itself is not newly discovered algebra.

## Ito, Takahashi, Ibi and Sampei, BiGaBP with non-orthogonal pilots

IEEE TCOM72(2),1045-1061,2024. DOI10.1109/TCOMM.2023.3325479.
Official record:
https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10287324/
Author institutional open-access record:
https://ir.library.osaka-u.ac.jp/repo/ouka/all/93323/

Its stated method relaxes large-system approximations and uses belief scaling under
short non-orthogonal pilots. Thus a broad claim that robust non-orthogonal bilinear
inference or relaxed AMP assumptions are new would fail. Its multiuser pilot setting is
not identical to same-delay operator coherence generated by CSTATE. We inspected primary
metadata/abstract here, not all17 pages; no claim of equation-by-equation nonoverlap.

## AFDM and shift-modulation JCED

The user's inspected novelty audit documents PBiGAMP-NDS, Wang etal., IEEE JOE2025,
DOI10.1109/JOE.2025.3565111, alongside AFDM PBiGaBP, fast structured PBiGAMP and time-domain
JCED. That archive already rules out broad claims to first delay/Doppler bilinear inference,
first time-domain computation or first FFT contractions. The audit is not a newly acquired
full-paper comparison in this turn.

## Most immediate competing implementations are INTERNAL

`fast_bilinear.py` has coherent off-diagonal self-feedback.
`block_bilinear.py` has full gain covariance and affine-complete reactions, including R.
They are real closest controls, not hypothetical future baselines. Their earlier tests
used unique working delays, as established by the source callers. Reusing them on the
new post-CSTATE geometry is a justified new test, not inventing them again.

## The only potential stronger scientific delta

An exact diagnosis of a newly exposed deterministic, order-one gain-mean feedback defect
under structural reopening, with a minimal scalar closure whose unchanged-regime behavior
and complete cost/reliability are tested. This changes the receiver map rather than only
accelerating an existing contraction. The outcome may support a narrow receiver method,
but it does not automatically exceed the fallback's novelty. If the final result is only
'restore a term that the old coherent implementation already computed', the algebraic
novelty is weak. A publishable core would require a substantial mechanism/theory/receiver
consequence demonstrated beyond one small diagnostic population. That has not been proved.

This is explicitly NOT evidence that the whole problem family is exhausted.
