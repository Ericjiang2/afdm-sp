2. **The paper already has an elegant core. Its reach ends before the decoder.** At exact duplication, a fixed aggregate gain \(H\) produces the same waveform. Yet splitting it equally cuts the scalar uncertainty penalty from \(u|H|^2\) to \(u|H|^2/2\). The exact same-delay block keeps that cost intact. The same geometry explains the opposite difficulty in sparse Bayesian learning: the waveform identifies the **sum** of the two gains while supplying no information about their **difference**. A separate coordinate ridge still rewards duplication slightly.

3. **Three ideas, in rank order.** The numerical cutoffs below are proposed bets. Freeze them before opening new outcomes. Use paired intervals clustered by physical frame. An interval spanning a cutoff is inconclusive.

   **① Make the grid finer and predict which receiver gets worse.** A support score that rewards two descriptions of one path should become easier to fool as a candidate approaches an occupied atom. **Prediction:** on fresh forced-miss frames, moving one same-delay competitor from 0.25 to 0.0625 Doppler bin from a strong anchor will increase its win rate against the same missing path by at least 10 percentage points more under scalar scoring than under `CODELAY`. **Check:** replay only the first exchange with the deletion, soft state, rival and number of candidates fixed. Both scorers see exactly the same pair. Cap at 5 core-hours. **Kill:** an upper 95% bound below 10 points defeats the quantitative claim. **Novelty:** coincidence invariance is already proved. The finite-spacing prediction on actual decoder states is new, and could fail for the same reason the ideal flip formula did.

   **② Test whether the low-pilot collapse is partly repairable.** Pilot energy buys a usable starting set of delays. Data energy buys a decodable frame once enough of those delays are correct. **Prediction:** at \(\rho=.15\) and 6–8 dB, raising the edit budget from two to four will rescue at least 10% of the two-edit failures that entered exchange missing three or four true delays, with a larger rescue fraction than in the matched \(\rho=.30\) group. **Check:** count eligible frames from saved starting supports first, then replay only those frames with the two budgets paired and all other settings frozen. Cap at 5 core-hours. **Kill:** an upper bound below 10% at .15, or a clear reversal of the between-fraction prediction. **Novelty:** the 93% residual-miss figure describes frames *after* failure. Predicting which frames benefit from two extra edits is new. The earlier null at .30 makes this a discriminating test.

   **③ Predict where one scalar stops being enough.** The chirp suppresses coupling between different delays, except near particular delay–Doppler combinations. **Prediction:** on a deliberately stressed candidate bank, the full covariance and `CODELAY` will choose different first edits at least 5 percentage points more often near resonance than off resonance. **Check:** use fresh soft states with a large first variance harmonic, selected without seeing winners. At the existing chirp setting, compare adjacent-delay trials near Doppler difference −5 bins with matched off-resonance trials, balancing the original `CODELAY` score margins. Score the first edit only, within 5 core-hours. **Kill:** an upper bound below 5 points. **Novelty:** the resonant matrix entry is already in Proposition 1. A consequential change in the winning edit is the new claim. This tests the search bank; the physical path pairs in the present channel stay outside that resonance.
