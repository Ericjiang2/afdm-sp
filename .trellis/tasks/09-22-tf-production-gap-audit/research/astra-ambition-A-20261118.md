## 1. What would make this a landmark paper?

**Confident.** The theory result would be a **weak-path recovery boundary for a receiver using decoder feedback**. Given a strong path, a missing weak path, and a finite Doppler search grid, predict when the receiver will spend its next support slot on a duplicate, when CODELAY will recover the weak path, and when neither receiver can distinguish the alternatives. The experimental result would be to **move a physical link across that boundary on purpose and predict the change before observing it**, including a regime where CODELAY’s advantage disappears. That pair would give another receiver designer a result they could use.

**Numerically checked.** The current evidence gets unusually close on one side of that boundary. On 7,667 failed frames, the scalar phantom reward had Spearman correlation 0.999 and median relative error 0.8%. Yet scalar phantom admission was still 78% *outside* the window, and the CODELAY residual reward had 90% median relative error. The missing quantity is the **margin between the winning phantom and the best available weak-path replacement**. A highly accurate prediction of one candidate’s reward can leave the sign of that margin unknown. The existing gain-map calculation also predicts much less variation with pilot fraction than the observed rescue gaps. **Derived — the starting point.** Freeze the decoder’s soft state. Let \(V\) be its symbol-error covariance, \(A_j\) a unitary path operator, and \(h\) a complex path gain. Replacing \(hA_j\) by \(k\) coincident copies of gain \(h/k\) preserves the channel operator \(H\), hence preserves \(\operatorname{tr}(HVH^H)\). The scalar uncertainty penalty falls from \(u|h|^2\) to \(u|h|^2/k\), where \(u=\operatorname{tr}V\). Grid refinement can therefore manufacture a finite apparent improvement with **zero change in the physical channel**. At \(u=0\) this particular advantage vanishes. A separate gain regularizer can still reward splitting, which is one reason a theorem promising zero CODELAY phantom admissions would be too strong.

**Derived — the quantity to predict.** Fix the retained support \(S\) after one deletion. Write the quadratic gain-fitting objective as \(c-2\operatorname{Re}(h^Hb)+h^HQh\), where \(b\) is the data correlation and \(Q\) includes the soft-mean Gram matrix, the chosen uncertainty block, and regularization. With \(Q\) positive definite, eliminating the retained gains gives the exact improvement from adding candidate \(j\):
\[
G_j=\frac{|b_j-Q_{jS}Q_{SS}^{-1}b_S|^2}
{Q_{jj}-Q_{jS}Q_{SS}^{-1}Q_{Sj}}.
\]
This expression exposes two ways a duplicate can win: a large residual correlation or a small denominator after accounting for the retained paths. Computing every \(G_j\) merely reruns the search. The scientific result would be to predict their **ordering** from a few physical and decoder-state quantities.

**Uncertain — the theorem worth trying to prove.** For a specified weak-path candidate neighborhood \(W\) and strong-path duplicate neighborhood \(D\), derive a finite-spacing bound on \(M=\max_{j\in W}G_j-\max_{j\in D}G_j\) for each arm. Start with the two-path margin and bound its perturbation using Doppler separation, residual correlation after fitting the retained paths, cross-delay coupling, and noise-estimation error. The bound must cover selection over the candidate bank, state when the weak candidate is present, and allow abstention near a tie. Its decisive prediction would be a region of **nonzero width at practical grid spacing** where \(M_{\rm scalar}<0<M_{\rm CODELAY}\). In the coincident, idealized limit it should recover your existing window. If the perturbation bound consumes that entire region, the theorem has failed its engineering purpose.

**Derived — one precision that matters for publication.** Under the full-block cyclic model and diagonal symbol variances, constant-modulus time-domain basis entries give each time sample variance \(u/N\). The co-delay trace is then the Fourier sum \(uD_N(\Delta\nu)\). Ordinary OFDM also satisfies this condition. AFDM’s chirp additionally shifts the **cross-delay** coupling in Doppler, with possible resonances. I would state those two facts separately. The project’s independent matrix calculation supports the distinction. **Confident — the decisive experiment.** Use a calibrated RF channel emulator so path identities and gains are known independently of either receiver. Move just the weak-tap power through the predicted transition while holding the strong tap, waveform, total transmit-power convention, and receiver settings fixed. Repeat at unseen Doppler offsets and pilot fractions with receiver-estimated noise. Freeze the theory, thresholds, and abstention rule first. Pair both arms on identical captures. At the first eligible support decision, compare them from the same saved decoder state and candidate bank. Then let each receiver finish normally and score every transmitted frame.

**Confident — what the headline figure should show.** Put strong/weak power ratio on the horizontal axis. Overlay the predicted transition band on measured probabilities of **duplicate admission, weak-delay recovery, and frame error**, with uncertainty clustered by independent channel realization. Include genuine, resolvable co-delay paths as a control: suppressing every second path at a delay can otherwise masquerade as success. The landmark outcome would be a correctly located transition on unseen captures, recovery of genuine doublets, and a reproducible reduction in pilot fraction at the same FER, code rate, and total transmit energy. A local transition that reproduces without an FER benefit would still establish the mechanism and would set a clear limit on its system value.

## 2. The bigger question and a multi-paper program

**Confident.** The bigger question is **what uncertainty must a decoder pass to a continuous-parameter channel estimator so that a physically equivalent channel gets the same score, and physically distinguishable channels get the right ordering?** Those are two different demands. A constant penalty satisfies the first and is useless for the second. This distinction gives the program three papers with separate results.

Support decisions under decoder uncertainty** | **Confident:** AFDM split pathology, finite-spacing recovery boundary, and prospective intervention above. | **Confident:** A frozen bound covers a substantial fraction of independent decisions and gets the sign right on both arms, including cases where it predicts no reversal. |
| **2. Minimum uncertainty information by waveform** | **Derived target:** For diagonal symbol variances \(v\) and a fixed candidate bank, stack the real and imaginary parts of the required uncertainty-block entries as \(Kv\). The minimum number of real **linear** summaries needed for exact reconstruction over all admissible \(v\) with nonempty interior is \(\operatorname{rank}K\). Sufficiency follows from a row-space basis. Necessity follows because any summary \(Tv\) must satisfy \(\ker T\subseteq\ker K\). The research contribution is to evaluate that rank for physical waveform families and identify which discarded directions change decisions. | **Confident:** Construct two admissible variance profiles with the same total \(u\) that reverse a support decision for a second waveform. Show that the extra waveform-specific summary restores it. A matrix discrepancy alone is weak evidence. |

Limits of decoder-assisted path resolution** | **Uncertain:** Derive a lower bound for distinguishing a genuine close doublet from a single path under a specified coded observation model, then exhibit a receiver approaching it. ## 3. What one PhD student can establish

- **Confident — realistic.** One student can derive the finite-spacing two- or three-path result, test whether it survives the actual eight-path candidate bank, and run the prospective emulator experiment given access to calibrated equipment. The first decisive check is a held-out sweep of **both candidate rewards at the same decision**. If the weak candidate is usually absent, or a third candidate usually wins, pivot to candidate generation. Tightening the phantom-reward formula would then address the wrong bottleneck.
- **Confident — realistic and independently valuable.** The linear-summary characterization and a two-waveform demonstration are within single-student reach. The first check is especially sharp: hold \(u\) fixed, change only the variance profile, and try to flip a real decision. If discarded variance directions never approach the observed decision margins, exact compression remains a mathematical result with limited receiver consequence.
- **Uncertain — a credible thesis stretch.** A sharp resolution lower bound with a matching receiver is plausible for one defined channel family, observation model, and decoder interface. The early test is whether a lower bound and an achievable boundary exhibit the same dependence on path separation or pilot energy. A wide gap leaves an open problem; it does not establish a fundamental receiver limit.
- **Confident — fantasy as stated.** A universal FER law from the scalar window alone. Two receivers can have the same frozen-state swap margin and different downstream decoding outcomes.
