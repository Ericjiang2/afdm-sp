2. **The structural fact.** **[D]** As the two Dopplers coalesce, their normalized difference approaches the *derivative* of a Doppler atom. Its shape survives even as its energy vanishes. For equal-amplitude rays opposite in phase at the frame center, correlation with a trial Doppler at offset \(f\) from their midpoint therefore approaches a multiple of \(K'_N(f)\), where \(K_N(f)=\sin(\pi f)/[N\sin(\pi f/N)]\) is the centered finite-frame kernel. In-phase rays instead approach an ordinary atom centered at zero. This follows directly from the project’s same-delay path operator. [Path model](.trellis/tasks/09-22-tf-production-gap-audit/research/proofs-20261119/theory-astra-v2.tex)

   **[N]** At \(N=1024\), \(|K'_N|\) peaks at \(|f|=0.66259\) bin. At the actual 0.35-bin separation, the opposite-phase optimum is 0.66483 bin from center. One atom there captures **57.4%** of the tap energy. The best atom anywhere between the true rays captures only **9.5%**. **[D]** This is a location prediction conditional on a detectable tap. At fixed ray amplitudes, exact coalescence cancels the signal.

3. **One candidate, with a receiver consequence.** **[D]** For equal ray amplitudes, separation \(0<\delta<1\) bin and frame-center phase difference \(\phi\), the one-atom fit improves as it moves outward past either true ray when
   \[
   \cos\phi<-K_N(\delta).
   \]
   The slope at the upper ray is proportional to \(K'_N(\delta)[K_N(\delta)+\cos\phi]\). This gives a different phase boundary at each spacing without fitting a “near” radius. The risky step is predicting that the noisy receiver’s initial estimate follows this isolated-tap geometry.
