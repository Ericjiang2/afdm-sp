SP-AFDM Bootstrapping Cliff

A superimposed-pilot AFDM receiver works well until its channel model has too many coefficients. Then it fails within a narrow range of K. This brief covers the model, where the question came from, what sets the edge, and a new law for how much information the receiver's first round delivers.

1 · Background

What the project studies

AFDM (affine frequency division multiplexing) places data on chirps whose slope is tuned to the channel. In a doubly dispersive channel, where every path has a delay and a Doppler shift, AFDM keeps paths apart in its chirp domain and so collects their diversity.

The receiver here uses a superimposed pilot (SP). Pilot and data occupy the same N chirp positions at the same time. The pilot takes a fraction ρ of the power and the data take a = 1 − ρ. Nothing is spent on guard intervals, but pilot and data interfere, so the receiver has to estimate the channel and the data together.

The paper line behind this work looks at channels where several rays share one delay bin with Doppler spread finer than one bin. The receiver models each delay tap with 8 DPSS (discrete prolate spheroidal) basis functions, so a channel with L taps costs K = 8L complex coefficients. Richer channels need larger K.

Question C1. How large can K grow before the SP receiver fails, and what physical quantity sets that limit?

2 · Modeling approach

Signal model and receiver loop

The received block is y = H(h)(p + x) + w, with pilot p, data x, noise power n = 10−SNR/10 and channel H(h) = Σk hk Bk. Because the channel is linear in its coefficients, H(h)s = A(s)h for an N × K matrix A(s) built from the transmitted block. Estimating h is a regression on A(p + x̂), which only works as well as the soft data x̂.

All numbers are in physical units: transmit power 1, channel energy 1, pilot power ρ, data power a.

3 · Origin of the problem

How the question sharpened

  1. A first-pass proxy. The early picture used an SINR threshold γ ≈ 1.37 for the first pass and a coefficient budget K* = κN, with a U-shape in channel richness. 0b1088b8, 35225b4c
  2. External review. Codex pointed out that inverting K* is classical SP-OFDM analysis (Liang, Rave, Fettweis). A cliff alone does not prove bootstrapping, and pilot columns are not orthogonal (largest correlation 0.150). 39eb9885
  3. Stored-row screen (T0). Near the cliff, round 1 almost never decodes. Frames that succeed are rescued by decoder feedback in later rounds, and realised channel energy dominates which frames fail. 32a62d27
  4. Scalar error model (T2), frozen before comparison. It failed. The round-1 channel error came out 1.55 to 3 times larger than predicted, so the one-dimensional error closure was dropped. 1dfb182c, 722a150e
  5. Change of coordinates (D2). Measured in information per coded bit, the code threshold is universal: I* = 0.577 for SP, true-channel and AWGN inputs alike. The old γ = 1.37 was an artefact of SINR-proxy units. ac08735a
  6. Interventions. More rounds move the cliff. An exact channel start is forgotten within round 1. Freezing the channel estimate fails (FER ≈ 1). bdad1a06
  7. Return map. Predictions frozen before the data arrived placed the cliff correctly for 1 to 4 rounds. 9473a774, 6b3ebda9
  8. Round-1 law (this brief). A derivation of I1(K), the one curve that was still only measured. 2d0a2f90
4 · Principle

Why the cliff happens

The state is the decoder's belief. The receiver rebuilds its channel estimate in every round and forgets how it started. What carries forward is the information the decoder hands back.

The code has a fixed threshold. Decoding succeeds about half the time once the decoder-input information reaches I* = 0.577 bit. The value comes from the code alone.

Each round is one step on a map. Round r + 1 delivers ΦK(Ir), starting from the round-1 value I1(K). As K grows, both the starting point and the whole map sink toward the diagonal. The gap between map and diagonal is a tunnel the iteration climbs through. A narrow tunnel needs more rounds, which is why the cliff seen with two rounds moves when the receiver runs four.

The tunnel closes at a fold. A denser map on 300 new frames (A4 run, 9 Oct) puts the average map below the diagonal at K = 224, 256 and 288, with intervals that exclude zero. From K = 224 the map has two stable states, a trap near 0.42 and the decoded state. An unstable point separates them and climbs toward I* as K grows (0.533, 0.547, 0.559). Ten-round runs from the pilot start stall: FER after round 10 is 0.61, 0.85 and 0.95 at K = 192, 224 and 256, and each frame ends either near 0.40 or near 0.78, with nothing in between.

The average map overstates long-run success near the fold. At K = 192 the average map is still open, yet 61% of frames stall. Ten-round runs put the cliff after ten rounds at K ≈ 180 (176 to 185), below the average-map fold near 210, and rounds after the fifth move it by less than 8. Frames differ, and each frame's own map, measured on the same frames, says who stalls: no frame whose own map stays below the diagonal up to I* decodes (0 of 459). An open map is needed but not enough. Only 29% to 49% of those frames decode, because the idealised map runs slightly above real trajectories.

One frame, two end states. For 62% to 70% of the frames that stall from the pilot start, an injected start below I* decodes within two rounds. Frames with no escape region below I* almost never do (1 of 150). So for one observation the outcome depends on where the decoder starts. Whether the stalled state is truly stable is not shown, and neither is whether a real receiver state can reach the decoded one: the injected start is an idealised decoder belief.

5 · Contributions

What is new and what is standard

Standard tools used here

  • EXIT charts and staircase prediction (ten Brink, 2001).
  • LMMSE channel estimation with soft data; training cost with K unknowns (Hassibi and Hochwald, 2003).
  • Bilinear message passing and its state evolution under random mixing (BiG-AMP, Parker, Schniter and Cevher, 2014).
  • Stein's identity linking a posterior mean's derivative to its posterior variance.
  • Density evolution for iterative SP-OFDM receivers (Liang, Rave, Fettweis), the closest prior work.

Candidate contributions

  • Established The SP receiver's code threshold is universal in information units. The SINR threshold used before was a unit artefact.
  • Calibrated K is the variable that drives the fold. The number of rounds sets where the finite-round cliff sits, and the channel start is forgotten.
  • Derived A closed-form round-1 law with a coherent self-absorption factor and a K/N load scaling.
  • Hypothesis The surviving coherent fraction equals the data share of the refit Gram matrix.

Novelty is not claimed yet. Before any novelty sentence, a literature pass will check the round-1 law against semi-blind and superimposed-training analyses and against OAMP and VAMP corrections for structured operators.

6 · First-principles model

How much information the round-1 loop delivers

Round 1 starts from a pilot-only channel estimate and runs 20 joint updates of channel and data with no help from the code. Its output information I1(K) sets the first step of every staircase. The derivation below finds the loop's fixed point in four steps. It uses only the SNR, ρ, K, N, the true-channel detector efficiency η and the code's AWGN curves.

Step 1 · Refit with soft data Derived · checked

Soft data with normalised error v act as extra pilot of energy a(1 − v), and their error adds noise av. A flat-prior LMMSE fit of K coefficients from N samples then leaves the channel error

eind(v)=Ks1+Ks,s(v)=n+av(ρ+a(1−v))N(1)

The per-coefficient shrinkage is β = 1/(1 + Ks). With v = 1 (pilot only) this reproduces the observed round-1 refit error within 3% in all 56 stored cells.

Step 2 · Detector link Derived

The LMMSE estimate is orthogonal to its error, so the estimated channel keeps energy 1 − e and the rest acts as interference. The detector's equivalent SINR and the soft-data error are

q(e)=ηa(1−e)n+e,v=MQPSK(q)(2)

η comes from the true-channel receiver, so e = 0 reproduces it exactly. The failed T2 model used ηa/(n + e) without the factor 1 − e, which matters once e reaches 0.2 to 0.4.

Step 3 · Self-absorption of the channel error Derived

This is the term the T2 model lacked. The detector's soft symbols are posterior means computed with the current, wrong channel. By Stein's identity, a posterior mean moves with its input at an average rate equal to posterior variance over noise variance. For QPSK that rate is d = qv. So the soft symbols carry a linear image of the channel error, d · GΔHs.

In the next refit that image lands in the residual as −d A(s)Δh. It lies in the span of the regressors, so the refit hands back a fraction of the old error:

Δhnew=cΔh+Δhfresh,c=κβd,0≤κ≤1(3)

The noise and data are the same on every pass, so the fresh part is the same vector each time and the amplitudes add. The fixed point is

e*=eind(v(e*))(1−c)2(4)

κ = 0 is the textbook picture in which soft-symbol errors are independent of the channel error; that is the failed T2 structure. κ = 1 is full coherent absorption. The two limits bracket the observed round-1 channel error in 56 of 56 stored cells, with no fitted parameter.

Step 4 · How much survives the receiver's correction Post-hoc hypothesis

The joint detector already carries Onsager-type correction terms. Those terms are exact only when every symbol mixes randomly with every coefficient. In SP-AFDM the coherent image is a fixed linear function of Δh, so the correction removes only part of it. The pilot regressors are exact and anchor the fit. The contamination enters through the soft-data regressors, so a natural guess weights it by their share of the refit Gram matrix:

κ=w(v)=a(1−v)ρ+a(1−v),I1(K)=JQPSK(q(e*))(5)

This choice came after fitting κ cell by cell to stored rows (with the T2 link): the fitted values ran from 0.31 to 0.97 and tracked ρ. It is a hypothesis. Section 7 measures c directly and tests the law on cells that have not been simulated.

What the law says

Each coefficient has an information cost. For small K, e* ≈ (K/N)Λ0. Λ0 lies between the genie-data cost n and the pilot-only cost (n + a)/ρ. Self-absorption roughly doubles it at 6.5 dB.

Only K/N matters. Every term depends on K and N through their ratio. If η and κ do not change with N, the round-1 cliff scales as Kc ∝ N. This is untested, because the frozen receiver accepts only N = 1024.

The round-1 loop never folds in this regime. The map has a single fixed point for every stored SNR and ρ up to K = 1024, and its slope there is at most 0.75 (median 0.17), so 20 updates converge. That explains why an exact channel start is forgotten. The sharpness of the cliff comes from the code's threshold, not from the loop.

The loop's gain fades with K. The pilot-only error saturates like Ks/(1 + Ks). The loop's own error keeps growing with K/N and is amplified by the falling SINR and by (1 − c)−2. The ratio of round-1 to pilot-only error therefore rises from 0.16 to 0.63, as measured.

Outcome (9 Oct). V3, the blind test, passed. On 20 cells nobody had simulated, the law predicted round-1 information to within 0.042 bit everywhere. The two limits held in every cell. The cliff came within 6% to 8% in two groups and 22% early at 6.5 dB, ρ 0.2, right on the bar. The law runs pessimistic throughout.

V2, the probe, rejected the mechanism. Nudging the converged channel shows a large loop gain (c ≈ 0.5 to 0.7) in the alternating detector and refit map, above the bound βd. At that gain the error would be 4 to 9 times the independent-error value. The receiver actually shows 1.2 to 1.7 times that value, measured against independent soft data of the same true quality. Its own corrections cancel about three quarters of the coherent gain. What remains acts like an effective κ between 0.21 and 0.56. It falls with ρ and sits near 0.7 w, so κ = w overstates it and is not the mechanism.

V1, the link check, failed one clause. The derived link is not reliably better than the old one when the channel error is small. Where the error is large, it is within 0.02 bit and the old link is off by up to 0.10. So the law stands as a calibrated predictor with one effective constant, not as a derived mechanism. Deriving that constant needs the receiver's joint updates.
