Rule

Above the upper boundary both prefer the duplicate. These are pairwise preferences. An accepted edit must also beat the incumbent and the candidates for other slots. Derived. The logged truth can label a newly proposed delay after prediction. It cannot tell the predictor which unoccupied delay is genuine. Conversely, maximizing every logged Schur gain after accounting for slot deletion would largely reproduce the receiver's decision. The following rule therefore withholds the measured Schur gain of every phantom candidate–slot pair. It uses measured gains for the competing nonphantom pairs. Its prediction tests the modeled phantom reward against the actual competition.
Assumed — candidate classes. Let F be the entering support and Sₛ=F∖{s} the survivors when slot s is removed. For each legal candidate c, assign class P if its delay occurs in Sₛ, N if its delay was absent from F, and O otherwise. Thus a change of Doppler on the uniquely dropped delay is O. Use the logged pre-edit fitted gains h̃ⱼ. Apply the rule separately to each arm.
Derived — finite-spacing score. Put T=E+u+λ. For a phantom candidate and its surviving same-delay anchor j, use the complex coherences κ=Bⱼ^{H}B_{c}/E and \(D=\operatorname{tr}(U_j^HU_c)/N_{\rm samples}\), both oriented from j to c. Set \(q_{\rm A2}=E\kappa\) and \(q_{\rm A1c}=E\kappa+uD\). On an isolated-anchor slice with bᵢ=Bᵢ^{H}y, b_{c}=κ^{*}bⱼ, and h̃ⱼ=bⱼ/T, the Schur reward is \[ \widetilde G_a(c,s)= \frac{|T\kappa-q_a|^2\,|\tilde h_j|^2}{T-|q_a|^2/T}. \] This keeps the physical ridge in both arms and the exact same-delay uncertainty term in A1c. It reduces to Eq. (13) when κ=D, and gives the Eq. (8) thresholds at coincidence. Numerically checked. Direct complex two-column ridge solves reproduced the reward within 9.1×10⁻¹⁷ in 12 algebraic cases. Assumed — decision rule. For each P pair use \(\widetilde G_a\), taking the strongest fitted surviving anchor on that delay. For every N or O pair use its logged arm-specific Schur gain \(G_a(c\mid S_s)\). Include all unoccupied delays so the strongest observed competitor can win. To compare different slots, subtract T|h̃ₛ|² from each gain. This is the exact deletion cost for a decoupled slot and a proxy on coupled supports. Let Pₐ,Nₐ,Oₐ be the maximum resulting net gains in the three classes, with an empty maximum equal to -∞.
Assumed — output and margin. Set \(K_a=T\max_{j\in F}|\tilde h_j|^2\) and τₐ=10⁻⁸Kₐ. Predict phantom if Pₐ>max(Nₐ,Oₐ,τₐ). Predict adds missing true delay if Nₐ>max(Pₐ,Oₐ,τₐ). Otherwise predict other, including no improvement and ties. A predicted new delay that proves false counts as an error. The A2 ranking score is \[ m_{\rm A2}=\frac{P_{\rm A2}-\max(N_{\rm A2},O_{\rm A2},\tau_{\rm A2})}{K_{\rm A2}} . \] The relative floor is a prediction convention. The receiver's exact acceptance threshold depends on its current objective.
Derived — residual limitation. The nonphantom Schur gains include the observed residual and interference. For phantom pairs, b_{c}-κ^{*}bⱼ can be nonzero, and the remaining paths can alter the fitted anchor gain and curvature. Eqs. (12)–(14) show why even the sign can then change. The listed truth-free inputs do not independently bound this complex residual tightly enough to certify a winner. The rule makes a falsifiable approximation at precisely this step. Eqs. Q2 — Assumed. Use one common subset for both arms, selected before examining either accepted edit. Require E,u,λ,Kₐ>0, legal P and N competitors, and the following checks on the pairs attaining \(P_{\rm A2}\) and \(N_{\rm A2}\). The phantom has one surviving anchor on its delay, |D|≥ .90, |κ-D|≤ .05, and absolute mean coherence at most .15 with every other survivor. The new-delay candidate has absolute mean coherence at most .15 with every entering atom. Break within-class ranking ties by slot, delay, then Doppler. Abstain outside this subset. These are observable geometry checks. A large unobserved projected residual on an admitted unit remains a prediction error.
Assumed — coverage forecast. 25% [5%, 50%] of cap-8 triggered units. This assumes that a close same-delay competitor is common and that isolation from the other atoms is the limiting requirement. The aggregates supply no estimate of the joint pass rate.

Falsifier
Q4 — Assumed. Coverage below 5%, an empty window stratum, or absence of either AUC class limits what can be scored. With adequate coverage and both outcome classes represented, a physical-frame-paired 95% interval for AUC wholly at or below .50 refutes the directional ranking prediction. An interval wholly at or below zero for the inside-window A2 minus A1c phantom-share difference refutes the predicted arm separation. A clear miss of a numerical forecast rejects that forecast. Failure on admitted units counts against the proposed receiver-level mechanism even if an omitted residual is a plausible explanation. The exact two-candidate identity has narrower premises.
Q5 — Derived. The always-phantom accuracy baseline is the observed A2 phantom fraction on the identical scoreable subset, with other and no-edit units retained. The brief's 6,997/7,462≈93.8% applies to all Q2 common triggers at cap 8. It cannot be substituted for that subset baseline. The rule must produce a positive paired accuracy gain to claim better classification. Binary AUC gives the complementary discrimination check: always-phantom has AUC .50 regardless of prevalence. A high AUC with no accuracy gain means the margin ranks the classes but its fixed decision threshold fails to beat the baseline. Limits
Derived. The 71 versus 1,366 rescues that gained a missing true delay are downstream rescue counts. They do not supply first-edit class counts, subset prevalence or window occupancy. Success here would support a conditional prediction of phantom reward. The rule uses logged nonphantom competition, so its accuracy cannot establish that the power-ratio window alone predicts the global edit.
